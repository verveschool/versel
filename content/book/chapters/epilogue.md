This is the standard we hold at VerveSchool.

It's not about being the most likeable. It's about caring enough to push people to be better than they think they can be.

It's about holding people accountable to excellence.

Being an operator is not a team. It's a mindset. It's a commitment to mastery. It's a refusal to be mediocre.

When you join us, you inherit a standard. The standard is that we don't accept excuses. We accept explanations. We learn from failure.

But we don't accept "that's just how it is" or "some people are just better at sales than others."

Some people are better at sales than others. And the reason is that they care more about being good than they care about being comfortable.

They're willing to be uncomfortable. They're willing to look at themselves honestly. They're willing to do the work.

That's the standard.

When you're part of a team that holds that standard, it changes you.

You stop accepting mediocrity in yourself. You stop making excuses. You start wanting to be excellent.

Not because anyone is forcing you. Because you've seen what's possible when people commit to mastery.

Operators protect each other. We push each other. We celebrate the wins. We learn from the losses. We hold the standard even when it's uncomfortable.

When someone on the team is slipping, we tell them. Not to be mean. To help them see.

When someone is getting sloppy, we call it out.

When someone is making excuses, we don't let them off the hook.

That's love. That's care. That's what a team looks like. The standard we hold is this.

You will record your calls. You will listen to them. You will get better.

You will study people better than you. You will ask for feedback. You will give feedback. You will care about outcomes.

If you're not willing to do those things, this isn't the place for you. Go somewhere easier. Go somewhere that asks less.

But if you're willing to do the work, if you're willing to care about excellence, if you're willing to push yourself harder than anyone else will push you, then you're home.

This is the place for people who are serious. This is the place for people who care about mastery.

This is the place for people who want to build something.

The world moves for those who do.

And those who do are the ones who care enough to get good.

The ones who refuse to accept their current version as their final version.

The ones who understand that mastery is not a destination. It's a direction.

And don't you dare settle for fine.
