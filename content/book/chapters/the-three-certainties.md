When someone buys something, three different things have to align at the same time. Most salespeople focus on one. That is why they are stuck.

## Product certainty

This is whether they believe the product works. They have to believe it will do what you say it does. This is the easiest certainty to build: show proof, reference case studies, and explain specifics.

But product certainty is not enough. A good product alone does not make people buy.

## Personal certainty

This is whether they believe you are not going to screw them. They have to believe you know what you are talking about and that you care whether this works for them. They have to believe you would tell them no if it was not right.

You build this by asking questions that come from experience, listening closely, being clear where your offer ends, and being willing to walk away.

## Decision certainty

This is whether they believe this is the right decision for them right now at this price. They have to believe the risk of buying is lower than the risk of not buying. This is where most sales die.

Build decision certainty by making the cost of inaction visible. Ask them to imagine life in two years if nothing changes. Ask what that costs in money, confidence, and respect. Connect your offer directly to the gap between where they are and where they want to be.

These certainties are not interchangeable. Ten out of ten on product with three out of ten on personal trust still loses. Ten out of ten on product and personal with two out of ten on decision certainty still loses.

You build all three simultaneously from the moment the conversation starts.
