When someone buys something, three different things have to align at the same time.

Most salespeople focus on one. That is why they are stuck. You need to understand all three and you need to understand how they interact.

Certainty number one: product certainty

This is whether they believe the product works.

They have to believe it will do what you say it does. They have to believe it works. They have to believe people use it and get results.

This is the easiest certainty to build. You show proof. You reference case studies. You give them specifics about how it works.

But here is what people get wrong. They think product certainty is enough. They think if the product is good enough, people will buy it.

That is not how it works.

Certainty number two: personal certainty

This is whether they believe you are not going to screw them.

They have to believe you know what you are talking about and that you care whether this works for them or not.

They have to believe you would tell them no if you genuinely thought it was not right.

And this only works if you mean it. You cannot fake it. If you are trying to close someone who is not a fit, it leaks through everything you say.

This certainty forms in how you interact, the questions you ask, how closely you listen, and whether you are here to help or to hit a quota.

People can sense the difference.

You build personal certainty by showing you have been here before. Asking questions that come from dealing with people like them.

Listening when they speak. Being clear where your offer ends. Being willing to walk away.

Certainty number three: decision certainty

This is whether they believe this is the right decision for them right now at this price.

They have to believe the value is worth the cost.

They have to believe that taking action is better than staying stuck. They have to believe the risk of buying is lower than the risk of not buying.

This is where most sales die.

How do you build decision certainty? You reveal the cost of inaction. You ask them to imagine their life in two years if nothing changes. You ask them what that costs in terms of money, confidence, and respect.

You connect what you are offering directly to the gap between where they are and where they want to be.

How they work together

Here is the critical part. These three certainties are not interchangeable.

If you have ten out of ten on product certainty and three out of ten on personal certainty, you lose. They do not trust you enough.

If you have ten out of ten on product and personal certainty but two out of ten on decision certainty, you lose. They think it is great but they do not think it is right for them right now.

You need all three at high levels at the same time.

Most salespeople build these linearly. They think you build product first, then personal, then decision. That is wrong.

You are building all three simultaneously from the moment the conversation starts.

Your opening establishes a baseline on personal certainty. The way you carry yourself. The tone of your voice. Whether you sound like an expert or a beggar.

Everything you say about the product is supposed to build product certainty while maintaining the personal connection.

You are not just listing features. You are connecting features to their specific situation.

Everything you do in the close is supposed to solidify decision certainty while making sure you have not lost ground on the other two.

Understand this and you understand why most salespeople fail.

They focus on product when the problem is personal certainty. Or they build personal certainty but never make the case for why this specific person must buy right now.

When you understand the three certainties, you have a framework for why some deals close and why some are lost.
