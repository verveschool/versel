Here's the problem with most salespeople. They think they're salespeople.

That's the mistake. That's what kills their ability to close.

The moment you think of yourself as a salesman, you start acting like one. You get nervous. You start trying to convince people.

You start pushing. You start sounding desperate. You put the person on the other side on defense.

The best salespeople don't think of themselves as salespeople at all.

They think of themselves as doctors.

A doctor doesn't beg a patient to take medicine.

There are no discounts on surgery because the patient is hesitant.

A doctor doesn't say "just trust me" and hope it works.

A doctor asks questions. A doctor runs diagnostics. A doctor tells you what's wrong. A doctor prescribes the solution.

The patient listens because the doctor has information they need.

That's the posture you need to take.

In the first four seconds of a call, the person on the other side decides if you're someone worth listening to or someone they need to escape from.

They're not deciding consciously. They're deciding from a primal place. Expert or beggar. Authority or supplicant. Guide or desperate.

If you sound like a doctor, they listen. They answer all your questions. They follow your lead.

If you sound like a salesman, they lie. They give you surface answers. They tell you they need to think about it just to get you off the phone.

The difference is not in what you know. It's in how you carry yourself.

A doctor carries certainty. A doctor carries the weight of having seen hundreds of cases. A doctor carries the assumption that they know something you need to know.

A salesman carries hope. Hope that this will go well. Hope that you like them. Hope that you'll say yes.

Hope is the enemy.

Here's what changes everything. You have to be willing to walk away.

A heart surgeon doesn't operate on a healthy heart just to bill insurance. A surgeon runs diagnostics. If surgery isn't the right move, they say so. They send you somewhere else.

If your prospect doesn't have a problem you can solve or they lack the drive to fix it, you walk away. You don't try to convince them. You don't lower your price. You don't stretch the truth about what you can deliver.

You say "I don't think this is the right fit" and you move on.

The moment you're willing to walk away, you stop being desperate. The moment you stop being desperate, you stop sounding desperate.

The moment you stop sounding desperate, people start wanting to work with you.

This is simple economics. Scarcity creates value. Your time is scarce if you're willing to spend it only on people who are good fits.

Your willingness to say no makes you infinitely more attractive to the people who will say yes.

So here's the first rule. Stop thinking of yourself as a salesman. Think of yourself as someone with information they need.

Think of yourself as someone who's seen this problem a hundred times. Think of yourself as someone who can help them move from where they are to where they want to be.

And be willing to tell them no if they're not ready. That's how you start.
