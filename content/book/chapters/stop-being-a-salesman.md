Here is the problem with most salespeople. They think they are salespeople.

That is the mistake. The moment you think of yourself as a salesman, you start acting like one. You get nervous. You start trying to convince people. You push. You sound desperate. You put the other person on defense.

The best salespeople think of themselves as doctors. A doctor does not beg a patient to take medicine. A doctor asks questions, runs diagnostics, tells you what is wrong, and prescribes the solution.

That is the posture you need to take. In the first four seconds of a call, the person on the other side decides if you are expert or beggar, authority or supplicant, guide or desperate.

If you sound like a doctor, they listen. If you sound like a salesman, they lie, give surface answers, and tell you they need to think about it just to escape.

A doctor carries certainty. A salesman carries hope. Hope that this will go well. Hope that you like them. Hope that you will say yes.

Hope is the enemy.

Here is what changes everything. You have to be willing to walk away. If your prospect does not have a problem you can solve or they lack the drive to fix it, you walk away. You do not lower your price or stretch the truth.

You say, “I do not think this is the right fit,” and you move on.

The moment you are willing to walk away, you stop being desperate. The moment you stop being desperate, people start wanting to work with you. Your willingness to say no makes you infinitely more attractive to the people who will say yes.
