When someone tells you they need to think about it or it is too expensive or they want to talk to someone first, most salespeople hear an obstacle.

That is the wrong interpretation entirely.

An objection is not an obstacle. An objection is a signal. It is telling you which part of their certainty is still too low.

But not all resistance is the same.

Some resistance is logical. Some resistance is emotional. One is loopable. The other is not.

If you misunderstand this, you will chase dead deals forever.

Objections are logic problems.

An objection is a genuine concern about the product itself.

"It is too expensive." "I am not sure the curriculum covers what I need." "I do not know if I can commit to two weeks."

These are questions about whether your solution solves their problem.

Objections are good news. They mean the person is thinking. They see value but they have a doubt.

Objections are loopable because the doubt is about specifics. You can address specifics.

Stalls are fear problems.

A stall is when someone is avoiding the decision.

"Let me think about it." "Send me more information." "I need to talk to my parents." "Let me check my calendar."

These are not objections about the product. These are delays. These are postponements.

These are ways of saying "I am not ready to commit right now" without saying no.

Stalls come from fear. Fear of making the wrong choice. Fear of commitment. Fear of change.

Stalls are not loopable because the problem is not with your logic or your offer.

If you loop a stall, you chase. You sound desperate. The prospect feels the energy shift and retreats further.

When someone stalls, you do not accept the first objection. You loop certainty.

You say:

"I understand. You want to think about it. Let me ask you this. You said you want [their goal in their words], right? And we talked about how this program gets you there by [what actually happens in simple terms], alright? So if that is where you want to go, and this is what gets you there, what happens if you wait six months?"

Pause. Let them answer.

They will tell you what they are actually scared of losing.

Then you ask:

"So knowing that, does it make more sense to start now or keep waiting?"

You are not telling them what to think. You are making them see what waiting costs.
