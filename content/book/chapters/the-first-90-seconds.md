The first 90 seconds decide whether you are worth listening to. Establish why you are talking, set a clear frame, and get permission to ask questions. Say who you are, reference the topic they checked for earlier, and explain that you want to understand their situation properly so you do not waste their time.

Then frame the call: you will ask questions, see whether you can help, and close it there if it is not a fit. This makes the conversation a consultation instead of a chase.

Ask a specific first question. Listen. Use small acknowledgments because they cannot see you nodding. Validate what they say, then follow up. By the end of 90 seconds, know whether they have a problem you can solve, whether you want to work with them, and what they care about beneath the surface.
