A close is not an event. It's an accumulation.

You build all the certainties perfectly and then most salespeople blow it at the last moment because they don't know how to ask for the money.

Here's why closes fail.

Asking too soon.

You ask for the sale before they're ready.

You've figured out the problem but you haven't amplified it enough. You've mentioned the solution but you haven't made the case for why they specifically must buy it.

You ask and they say "let me think about it." You've invited them to delay.

Asking the wrong question.

Instead of asking for commitment, you ask a question that invites negotiation.

"So what do you think?" or "Does that sound interesting?" or "Would you want to move forward?"

All of these are soft. All of these invite objections.

Instead of "What does your schedule look like this week?" which is asking for action.

Asking like you're unsure.

You end the sentence with uptalk. You use qualifiers. "If you want to" or "If you think it makes sense."

You sound like you're hoping. You sound desperate.

The certainty you built gets erased the moment you're supposed to execute.

Asking and then talking.

You ask them "Do we get started?" and then before they can answer, you keep talking.

You're filling the silence because silence makes you uncomfortable.

The moment they were about to say yes, you talked them into reconsidering.

Silence after a close is sacred. You ask. You wait. You don't interrupt.

Not asking at all.

You spend an hour building perfect certainty and then at the end you say "Okay, well let me send you more information and you can think about it."

You don't ask. You don't commit. You leave them on the hook indefinitely.

This is the worst outcome. Now you have to follow up and the energy is gone.

What a clean close sounds like.

"Based on everything we've talked about, I think this is the right move for you. And I want to work with you. So here's what needs to happen. You're going to come in next Monday. We'll run through onboarding. You'll meet the team. We'll get started the following week.

What does that schedule look like for you?"

Notice what you've done.

You've stated that it's the right move. You've stated that you want to work together. You've told them what's going to happen.

And then you've asked a logistical question, not a commitment question.

"What does your schedule look like?" is not asking "Do you want to do this?"

It's assuming they do and asking for logistics.

When they start answering the logistical question, they've answered the question. They've committed.

When they object again.

Sometimes they'll hit you with an objection.

"I still need to think about it" or "It's too much money right now."

This is the final test. This is where you don't give ground.

You loop once. You address the objection with the mechanical loop. You ask again.

If they object again, you do something different.

You tell them the truth.

"I get it. And I respect that. But here's what I also know. You came to me because something needs to change. You said you can't keep doing what you're doing. You said the situation is costing you. All of that is still true right now.

So the question isn't whether you will do this. The question is whether you're ready to stop suffering.

And if the answer is not right now, that's okay. You can come back when you're ready.

But don't tell yourself you don't have a choice. You do. You're choosing to stay stuck if you don't move."

This is not mean. But it does expose what's already happening.

And exposure in the close creates more closures than pushiness ever could because people respect you for naming what they're running from.

If they still say no, you let them go. You don't chase. You don't negotiate down. You don't try another angle.

You say, "Okay, I respect that. But I'm going to stay in touch. Sometimes people realize a few weeks later that they made the wrong call.

If that happens, reach out."

And you move on.

The close with integrity is the hardest close because it requires that you don't need the sale.

That you would rather not close someone who's not ready.

That you believe in what you're selling enough to know it will help the people who commit.

Once you have that mindset, the close becomes simple. It's not a technique. It's an honest conversation with someone about what they need to do to solve their problem.
