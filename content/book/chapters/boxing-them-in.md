---
title: "Boxing them in"
description: "How to systematically destroy their backup plans so they have to admit that something serious needs to change."
order: "7"
part: "The mechanics"
---

This is the move most salespeople miss.

Once you've figured out the current state and they've acknowledged the problem, most salespeople move immediately to presenting the solution.

Amateur move.

What you do instead is box them in. You make them admit that their current alternatives don't work.

You methodically destroy their backup plans so they have to admit that something has to change and it has to be serious.

This does two things at once.

It proves the problem is painful and unsolvable with what they've been trying. And it creates pressure for them to consider taking action instead of staying stuck.

Here's how you do it.

Start with what they've tried. "You mentioned you've been looking for better opportunities. What have you done?" They tell you. "And what happened?" They answer. "So that approach didn't work."

Now box them in on their other options.

Option one is the free route. YouTube, self teaching, online resources.

"You could do what most people do. Spend six months or a year watching YouTube videos and teaching yourself. No money out of pocket. The question is, have you done that? And if you did, what happened? Did you finish? Did it lead to a job?"

They usually say no or they're still not sure.

Then you go for it. "So you've been thinking about this for how long? And you haven't pulled the trigger on free learning yet. Why do you think that is? And more importantly, if free learning was going to work for you, wouldn't you have done it already?"

This isn't mean. It's accurate. You're holding up a mirror to their inaction.

Option two is the long road. College, degree, formal program.

"Another path is the traditional route. Go back to school. Two years minimum. Ten to fifteen lakhs. You could do that. Do you have two years? Do you have that money right now? And are you ready to go back to being a student?"

They realize they're not. The pieces don't fit.

Option three is solo. Figuring it out on your own.

"You could also try to figure this out completely solo. Learn by doing. Find a job and fumble your way through. The question is, do you think that's going to work? Are you someone who can force yourself to learn hard things when nobody's holding you accountable? Most people can't. Most people need structure and someone who's been through it."

Option four is staying where you are.

"Or you could do nothing. Keep your current job, keep making what you're making, keep feeling how you feel. Stay stuck. What happens in six months? What happens in a year? Where are you in three years if nothing changes?"

Watch what happens.

You've systematically eliminated every alternative.

Free learning won't work because they would have done it. Formal education takes too long and costs too much.

Solo requires discipline they probably don't have. Staying the same is actively harmful.

Suddenly the picture is clear. The only viable option is something structured, guided, and something like what you're offering.

This is consulting through elimination.

You're showing them what's possible given their constraints. You're not suggesting they buy. You're suggesting they face reality.

You do this before you present your solution. You're asking them to agree with logical statements about why other things won't work.

They're not agreeing to buy. They're agreeing with facts. They're finally accepting the reality.

Once they've admitted their alternatives don't work, when you present your solution, they're primed. They're ready. They've already eliminated the other options.
