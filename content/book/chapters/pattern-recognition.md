Great closers have speed of diagnosis.

They walk into a conversation and in the first five minutes they know. They know whether this person is going to buy. They know what the blocker is. They know exactly what needs to happen to move them forward.

They are fast because they have seen the pattern a hundred times.

There are patterns in how people think. Patterns in how they stall. Patterns in what moves them.

The person who is stuck and desperate to get out is different from the person who is complacent and thinks everything is fine.

The person who is greedy for upside is different from the person who is scared of downside.

The person who is skeptical because they have been burned before is different from the person who is skeptical because they do not believe they are capable.

Each pattern requires a different diagnosis.

A stuck person needs to feel the cost of staying. A complacent person needs to feel the loss of possibility. A greedy person needs to feel scarcity. A scared person needs to feel certainty.

If you diagnose wrong, you treat wrong. You end up pushing the wrong lever.

Building pattern recognition is simple but not easy.

You record your calls. You review them. You notice what questions work. You notice what creates movement.

You study your wins. You study your losses. You get feedback from people better than you.

You build an internal database of patterns.

Over time, what was conscious competence becomes unconscious competence. You stop thinking about it. You just know.

And when you know, you move fast and you close more.
