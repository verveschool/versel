Before anyone buys anything from you, they are already skeptical.

This isn't a problem you created. This is a problem that exists in the world.

Understanding the different types of skepticism matters because each type requires a different solution.

Practical skepticism

This is the easiest type. They've been burned before. They've bought things that didn't work. They've wasted money on courses that didn't move the needle.

Their skepticism is based on experience.

This is good news. If you ask them what went wrong, they'll tell you. If you listen and genuinely understand why it didn't work, you can position yourself as different.

Not because you say so. Because you understand what failed and you know why what you're offering works differently.

Social skepticism

They've been taught their whole life that salespeople lie.

They've heard stories. Salespeople are out for themselves. They only care about commission.

This skepticism is rooted in reality. Because there are salespeople who lie. So when you show up, they have to assess whether you're one of those or whether you're different.

You prove you're different through actions. You ask questions instead of pitching. You listen instead of performing. You're honest about what you can't do. You're willing to say no.

Psychological skepticism

This is the one most people miss.

When someone is considering a decision, they're running a calculation that's deeper than price and features.

They're calculating risk. What if I do this and it doesn't work? What if I spend the money and time and nothing changes? What if everyone else can do this but I can't?

If the perceived risk is higher than the perceived reward, they won't buy.

It's not logic. It's survival instinct. Their nervous system is trying to keep them safe.

Here's what's critical. This skepticism is not personal to you. It's not because they don't like you. It's because their nervous system is doing its job.

When you understand that, you stop taking objections personally. You start seeing skepticism as data.

It's telling you which part of their certainty is still too low.

How to handle it

Skepticism doesn't evaporate. It gets replaced by a stronger belief or it lingers and kills the deal.

Your job is to methodically replace skepticism with certainty.

You do this by understanding which skepticism you're dealing with. Are they skeptical about the product? About whether they can trust you? About whether they can execute?

Each requires a different approach.

If someone doesn't believe the product works, no amount of trust will move them. If someone doesn't trust you, no amount of product information will move them.

If someone doesn't believe they can execute, no amount of proof will move them.

Consult to figure out which one you're dealing with. Then solve for that.

That's the work. Understanding and replacing skepticism with certainty.
