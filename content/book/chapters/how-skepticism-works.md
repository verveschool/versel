Before anyone buys anything from you, they are already skeptical.

This is not a problem you created. This is a problem that exists in the world.

Understanding the different types of skepticism matters because each type requires a different solution.

**Practical skepticism**

This is the easiest type. They have been burned before. They have bought things that did not work. They have wasted money on courses that did not move the needle.

Their skepticism is based on experience.

This is good news. If you ask them what went wrong, they will tell you. If you listen and genuinely understand why it did not work, you can position yourself as different.

Not because you say so. Because you understand what failed and you know why what you are offering works differently.

**Social skepticism**

They have been taught their whole life that salespeople lie.

They have heard stories. Salespeople are out for themselves. They only care about commission.

This skepticism is rooted in reality. Because there are salespeople who lie. So when you show up, they have to assess whether you are one of those or whether you are different.

You prove you are different through actions. You ask questions instead of pitching. You listen instead of performing. You are honest about what you cannot do. You are willing to say no.

**Psychological skepticism**

This is the one most people miss.

When someone is considering a decision, they are running a calculation that is deeper than price and features.

They are calculating risk. What if I do this and it does not work? What if I spend the money and time and nothing changes? What if everyone else can do this but I cannot?

If the perceived risk is higher than the perceived reward, they will not buy.

It is not logic. It is survival instinct. Their nervous system is trying to keep them safe.

Here is what is critical. This skepticism is not personal to you. It is not because they do not like you. It is because their nervous system is doing its job.

When you understand that, you stop taking objections personally. You start seeing skepticism as data.

It is telling you which part of their certainty is still too low.

**How to handle it**

Skepticism does not evaporate. It gets replaced by a stronger belief or it lingers and kills the deal.

Your job is to methodically replace skepticism with certainty.

You do this by understanding which skepticism you are dealing with. Are they skeptical about the product? About whether they can trust you? About whether they can execute?

Each requires a different approach.

If someone does not believe the product works, no amount of trust will move them. If someone does not trust you, no amount of product information will move them.

If someone does not believe they can execute, no amount of proof will move them.

Consult to figure out which one you are dealing with. Then solve for that.

That is the work. Understanding and replacing skepticism with certainty.
