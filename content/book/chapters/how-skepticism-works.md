Before anyone buys anything from you, they are already skeptical. Understanding the different types of skepticism matters because each type requires a different solution.

## Practical skepticism

They have been burned before. They bought things that did not work. Ask what went wrong. Listen. Then position yourself as different because you understand what failed and why your approach works differently.

## Social skepticism

They have been taught that salespeople lie. You prove you are different through actions: ask questions instead of pitching, listen instead of performing, be honest about limitations, and be willing to say no.

## Psychological skepticism

This is the one most people miss. The buyer is calculating risk: what if this does not work, what if I waste the money, what if everyone else can do this but I cannot? If perceived risk is higher than perceived reward, they will not buy.

Skepticism is not personal. It is data. It tells you which part of their certainty is still too low.

Your job is to methodically replace skepticism with certainty. If they do not believe the product works, no amount of personal trust will move them. If they do not trust you, no amount of product information will move them. If they do not believe they can execute, no amount of proof will move them. Consult to find the gap, then solve for that.
