Most founders are not entrepreneurs.

And many entrepreneurs are not founders.

Because entrepreneurship is not about starting a company.

It is about accepting responsibility for outcomes you cannot control.

When your decisions work, you benefit.

When they don't, you bear the cost.

No hiding.

No blaming.

No guaranteed reward for trying.

A founder can spend years protected by funding, a salary, or the option to walk away.

A salesperson on commission, a freelancer, or a small business owner may have no such protection.

The title is different.

The exposure is not.

This is why entrepreneurship is a way of operating, not a position.

It begins the moment your decisions have real consequences for you.

Starting a company can be entrepreneurial.

Working inside one can be entrepreneurial.

Owning a small shop can be entrepreneurial.

The label doesn't matter.

The ownership does.

Founding is an event.

Entrepreneurship is accepting that the outcome is yours.
