Sales is computationally irreducible.

You can’t predict a close from a lead score or a LinkedIn profile. Those are just compressed signals, not the full computation.

The only way to know is to run the process. You have to have the conversation.

Why this matters. Stephen Wolfram showed that complex systems have no shortcuts. Markets, minds, buying decisions. No formula reaches the outcome faster than executing every step.

The founder who runs more conversations doesn’t get luckier. They simply compute further along the irreducible path. They discover signals others never see.

How this is won:

- Treat profiles as prompts, not answers.
- Make the calls.
- Track booked meetings and next steps.
- Build tight feedback loops.
- Don’t always trust gut feel.

Iterate on the questions you ask, not the stories you tell.

Keep inputs clean. Lies and fluff corrupt the signal and destroy learning. Scale the process that generates data, not the data itself.

On leverage: Playbooks only become valuable when tested at volume. Until you run them at scale, they remain unproven theory.

Clever scripts don’t win. Discipline to run more iterations and learn faster does.

Stop scoring deals in the abstract. The world moves for those who do.
